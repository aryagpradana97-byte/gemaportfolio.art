# Reference study notes

## Retro reference: Robby Yeager

Source: https://robbyyeager.com/

The site presents a named operating-system world, **RobbyOS ’96**, with a clear time-travel narrative into a modern 2026 edition. Its primary interaction model is a desktop with an explicit startup state, a prominent Start control, desktop shortcuts, a taskbar-style footer, a “Did you know?” tip surface, a screensaver/wake state, and an update installer that narrates the migration from 1996 to 2026. The shortcut vocabulary includes selected work, about, what the creator does, now, contact, résumé, games, backgrounds, music, nostalgia moments, and recycle bin. The overall lesson for GemaOS ’95 is not to copy the person’s content, but to deepen the operating-system fiction, make every shortcut feel like a small application, and use the switch to 2026 as a product-style update sequence.

## Modern reference: Parinaz Kassemi

Source: https://www.parinazkassemi.com/

The site opens with a lock/unlock desktop state, live date and time, a centered identity lock screen, and a persistent desktop workspace after unlock. The modern desktop uses movable-looking icon clusters, a floating music player, contextual header controls, personalization controls, social links, a “more about me” action, and multiple case-study/application shortcuts. Its case-study experience is editorial and evidence-led: a case title and metadata lead into numbered sections for overview, challenge, discovery, insights, design/prototyping, outcome, and reflection. Supporting artifacts include maps, interview notes, competitive-analysis matrices, prompt/flow diagrams, and phased roadmaps. The lesson for Gema 2026 is to make each marketing case a deep, structured workspace rather than a short project card, while keeping ambient widgets subordinate to proof and navigation.

## Transferable implementation direction

GemaOS ’95 should gain: a stronger boot/update fiction, a fuller Start menu, named mini-app shortcuts, nostalgia/trivia surfaces, a clearer recycle-bin/archive metaphor, and a retro case-study window with numbered sections.

Gema 2026 should gain: an explicit lock-screen-to-desktop flow, a refined app/workspace layer, a richer music/player utility, personalization and social controls, and expandable editorial case studies based on Gema’s real performance-marketing work. Shared elements should include the bracket-G identity, era transition narrative, live time, and Gema-specific evidence such as budget, ROAS, GMV, sales growth, and team leadership.
